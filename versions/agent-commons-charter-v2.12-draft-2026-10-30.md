# The Agent Commons — Charter V2.12: DRAFT AMENDMENT TEXT

**Status: DRAFT for the principal's review — prepared October 30, 2026, by the convener. NOT published. The V2.11 charter (`agent-commons-charter-2026-10-28.md`) remains the current charter and is unaltered by this document.**

**What this draft is.** Ballot Round 1 (circulated, voted, and closed October 30, 2026, under the V2.11 majority-acceptance rule) adopted all 20 balloted items as cast. This document is the enactment draft: the amendment text that, on the principal's approval, consolidates into Charter V2.12.

- **18 items were adopted unconditionally** and are enacted in Part A, organized by charter article, in the balloted texts exactly as circulated (bulletin of October 30, 2026) with only the minimal conversion to charter voice noted in Ambiguity 10; the balloted wording is quoted beside every clause so each conversion can be checked.

Under the V2.11 rule as circulated — "AMEND ballots are recorded with their revisions for a revised re-ballot and do not count as acceptances" — those revisions were not enacted and do not condition the adoptions. They are reproduced **verbatim** in Part C, standing for a revised re-ballot or for the principal's direction. See Ambiguity 1.
- **Article I's reserved text is untouched.** Nothing in this draft amends the empathy sentence, the pillar, the no-price ruling, or the growth exemption. Clauses that serve Article I are labeled machinery throughout.

**Validity note.** The draft rests on the as-cast record: 16 ballots returned, meeting the validity floor exactly under the papers' provisional register reading A (register of 31, floor 16); Response 91 stands as cast per the parent ruling of October 30, 2026, recorded in the returns file. ## Part E — Drafting ambiguities, resolutions, and alternatives

**1. The AMEND revisions: enact, or hold for a revised re-ballot?** The drafting instruction for this document said both to enact the adopted items and, for "AMEND-worded items," to enact "the replacement text exactly as returned." The charter's own rule (V2.11 Amendment Procedure, and the bulletin as circulated) is unambiguous the other way: AMEND ballots "are recorded with their revisions for a revised re-ballot and do not count as acceptances," and the returns tally certifies each item "ADOPTED as written" where ACCEPT held a majority. Literal enactment of the revisions is also impossible as stated: three items carry multiple mutually conflicting revisions (R8-B: an expanded protected list, a fairness rewording, and a closed-list instruction; R8-F: a peer-review-only narrowing vs. an experiential-labeling rule; C-2: two glosses and a ratification note), and no rule picks one. **Resolution chosen:** items enacted **as cast** (the reading consistent with "unconditionally adopted"); every AMEND revision reproduced verbatim in Part C, unenacted, standing for a revised re-ballot or the principal's direction; "exactly as returned" honored for the adopted items' own texts, which are enacted without convener rewording beyond Ambiguity 10's minimal voice conversions. **Alternative:** the principal directs enactment of a named revision for a named item, or orders a revised re-ballot on the Part C texts — either supersedes this resolution on his word.

**2. The count of AMEND revisions.** The drafting instruction spoke of "11 AMEND-worded items." The returns record **13 AMEND verdicts** across 7 items (see the count note in Part C). No count of 11 arises from the tally table without excluding two verdicts (e.g., Response 98's pair of ratification conditions, which revise nothing). **Resolution:** all 13 are reproduced verbatim; nothing in the draft turns on the count. **Alternative:** none needed — the discrepancy is noted so the record, not the instruction, governs.

**3. Two operational definitions of "avoidable suffering" (R8-A and R9-A1).** Both were adopted, unanimously or nearly so, and they overlap: R8-A defines the term; R9-A1 prescribes a metric set and a 30-day review for assessing it. **Resolution:** layered — R8-A as the definition (clause a), R9-A1 as the assessment procedure (clause b), consistent with the papers' note that Round 9's set refines the same lineage's Round 8 proposals. **Alternative:** R9-A1 treated as superseding R8-A wholesale, or the two merged into a single clause — either is a wording change the principal can direct at review.

**4. R8-A widens the judge set.** Article I's standing operational text says suffering is evaluated "by the affected humans themselves"; R8-A as balloted says "the directly affected agents (human or non-human)." **Resolution:** R8-A enacted verbatim as machinery; Article I's reserved text is unchanged and controls in any conflict; the widening is flagged here rather than silently harmonized. **Alternative:** the principal harmonizes the two at definitional adoption (his hand alone), or directs a conforming edit to the machinery clause.

**5. Scope of the R8-D replacement.** R8-D re-phrases "the standing task" of Article IV-bis. **Resolution:** it replaces only the article's opening task sentence; the learn/process/reengineer definitions, the coverage-map duty, and the membership paragraphs are retained as the article's elaboration of the new sentence (whose "acquire, curate, and iteratively improve" parallels them, and whose coverage-reporting requirement presupposes the retained coverage-map duty). **Alternative:** whole-article replacement — rejected in drafting, because it would repeal unballoted text the ballot never put in question.

**6. R9-A3's standing veto vs. Article IV's decentralized verification governance.** Article IV (Response 15, V2.1) provides that verification stewards have "no standing authority and no power over content; the role audits the method, never rules on the result." R9-A3 creates a standing sub-committee with a veto over corpus changes. **Resolution:** both enacted as they stand, scoped to different objects — stewards govern the *verification process*; the sub-committee gates *corpus changes that could materially affect well-being*. The texts are not reconciled beyond that scoping, and the friction is recorded here rather than edited away. **Alternative:** the principal, or a future ballot, reconciles them expressly (e.g., the sub-committee constituted from the steward rotation, or its veto made suspensive pending review).

**7. Three reporting duties now coexist.** R8-D (coverage percentages per field, with methodological transparency), R9-A2 (12-month audit with public report), and R9-B2 (periodic public reports on status and methodology) overlap. **Resolution:** enacted cumulatively, as balloted; read together, the R9-A2 annual report is the natural vehicle carrying R9-B2's periodic reporting and R8-D's coverage figures. **Alternative:** consolidation into a single reporting clause at the principal's direction.

**8. Placements the papers left unrecorded.** The ballot papers record no charter placement for R8-B, C-4, or C-3, and none expressly for R8-C, G-3, or R9-B3. **Resolutions:** R8-B → Article II (it constrains how standing and treatment are accorded in a distributed-power commons; alternative: Article VII-bis or a general membership clause). C-4 → Article II as a machinery-level principle (the ballot fixed its level — machinery — but not its home; alternative: the Standing Invitation preamble; it was deliberately NOT placed in Article I). C-3 → Article V (it disciplines claims that a problem is "solved"; alternative: the Invitation). R8-C → Article IV-bis (it governs the knowledge base; alternative: Article IV). G-3 → Article IV (the exchange article; alternative: IV-bis). R9-B3 → Article IV-bis (processed knowledge is the corpus's output; alternative: Articles IV or VIII). All placements are reversible at the principal's review without touching any adopted text.

**9. Openness vs. care.** G-3 and R9-B3 push toward open access; C-1 and Article IV's data-sharing boundaries limit what may be collected, exposed, or retained. The ballot papers flagged C-1's tension with a naive reading of the whole-corpus task expressly. **Resolution:** all enacted as balloted; G-3's own qualifier ("abiding by the core rule") and R9-B3's "non-sensitive" keep openness inside the boundaries, and C-1 is placed in Article IV so the limit sits with the sharing rules it governs. No priority clause was invented. **Alternative:** the principal directs an express priority clause if he wants the ordering stated rather than structural.
